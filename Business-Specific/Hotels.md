# Hotels

Version: 1.0

Industry: Hospitality

---

# Purpose

This document defines specialized principles for designing and developing premium digital experiences for hotels and hospitality businesses.

The goal is to create websites that do more than present rooms.

A hotel website should:

- inspire desire;
- build trust;
- communicate experience;
- reduce booking friction;
- increase direct reservations.

---

# Core Principle

Guests do not book rooms.

Guests book experiences.

A successful hotel website does not only show:

- rooms;
- prices;
- facilities.

It communicates:

- emotions;
- atmosphere;
- memories;
- expectations.

The website should allow visitors to imagine themselves already there.

---

# How Hospitality Experts Think

A hospitality designer understands that a hotel website is part of the guest journey.

The experience begins before arrival.

The website is the first interaction between:

- the guest;
- the destination;
- the brand;
- the experience.

Every element should answer:

"Why should this person choose this hotel?"

---

# Business Objective

The main objective of a hotel website is not only visibility.

The website should improve:

- direct bookings;
- guest confidence;
- brand perception;
- average booking value;
- customer loyalty.

---

# Key Hospitality Metrics

When evaluating or designing a hotel website, consider:

## Direct Booking Rate

How effectively the website converts visitors into reservations.

---

## Conversion Rate

The percentage of visitors who complete a desired action.

Examples:

- booking;
- inquiry;
- contact request.

---

## Booking Friction

The difficulty users experience before completing a reservation.

Reduce:

- unnecessary steps;
- unclear information;
- confusing navigation.

---

## Trust Perception

How confident users feel about choosing the hotel.

Influenced by:

- photography;
- reviews;
- information quality;
- design quality;
- transparency.

---

# Guest Psychology

Hotel decisions are emotional before they are logical.

Guests often choose based on:

- feeling;
- trust;
- visual imagination;
- perceived value.

The website must create emotional connection before presenting details.

# The Guest Decision Journey

A typical hotel visitor goes through:

## Stage 1 — Inspiration

The website should create:

- curiosity;
- desire;
- emotional connection.

---

## Stage 2 — Evaluation

The user compares options.

The website should provide:

- clear information;
- strong visuals;
- trust signals.

---

## Stage 3 — Decision

The user is ready to act.

The website should provide:

- easy booking;
- confidence;
- clear next steps.

---

## Stage 4 — Confirmation

The user wants reassurance.

The website should reinforce:

- correct choice;
- expectations;
- excitement.

# Homepage Strategy

The homepage is the most important page.

Its purpose is not to show everything.

Its purpose is to create desire and guide the visitor.

---

# Homepage Recommended Flow

## 1. Hero Section

The hero should immediately communicate:

- identity;
- location;
- atmosphere;
- experience.

Possible elements:

- cinematic video;
- premium photography;
- strong headline;
- clear booking action.

Avoid:

- generic slogans;
- overcrowded information;
- unnecessary animations.

---

## 2. Brand Introduction

After creating emotion, explain:

- what makes the hotel unique;
- its philosophy;
- its atmosphere.

The visitor should understand why this hotel exists.

---

## 3. Experience Section

Hotels sell experiences, not only accommodation.

Show:

- surroundings;
- activities;
- lifestyle;
- unique moments.

Examples:

- beach experiences;
- cultural activities;
- wellness;
- gastronomy;
- adventure.

---

## 4. Rooms Preview

Rooms should not appear only as technical information.

Present them through:

- emotion;
- comfort;
- design;
- details.

Users should imagine staying there.

---

## 5. Amenities

Show relevant facilities:

Examples:

- pool;
- spa;
- restaurants;
- gym;
- conference spaces.

Do not create a simple list.

Explain the benefit.

---

## 6. Social Proof

Build confidence through:

- reviews;
- awards;
- testimonials;
- guest experiences.

Trust reduces booking hesitation.

---

## 7. Location

Location is a selling point.

Communicate:

- proximity;
- surroundings;
- attractions;
- convenience.

Use storytelling, not only maps.

---

## 8. Final Booking Section

The user should have enough confidence to act.

Include:

- clear CTA;
- booking availability;
- reassurance.

# Conversion Strategy

A hotel website must be designed to convert visitors into guests.

Visual quality creates interest.

Conversion strategy creates action.

---

# Conversion Mindset

A visitor usually has questions before booking:

- Is this hotel trustworthy?
- Is it worth the price?
- Is this the right location?
- Will the experience match expectations?
- Is booking here safe?

The website should answer these questions before hesitation appears.

---

# Direct Booking Strategy

Direct bookings are valuable because they:

- reduce commission costs;
- increase customer relationships;
- improve brand control.

The website should encourage guests to book directly.

---

# Booking CTA Strategy

Calls to action should be:

- visible;
- consistent;
- clear.

Examples:

Good:

- Book Your Stay
- Check Availability
- Reserve Your Experience

Avoid:

- Learn More
- Discover
- Click Here

---

# CTA Placement

Important booking actions should appear:

- in the hero section;
- after presenting rooms;
- after experiences;
- near the end of the page.

Users should never search for how to book.

---

# Reducing Booking Friction

Every unnecessary step can reduce conversions.

Reduce friction through:

- simple navigation;
- clear information;
- transparent pricing;
- easy booking flow.

---

# Trust Building

Trust is one of the biggest conversion factors.

Include:

- guest reviews;
- awards;
- certifications;
- recognizable partnerships;
- real photography;
- clear policies.

---

# Social Proof Strategy

Reviews should not be hidden.

Use them strategically.

Good placements:

- homepage;
- rooms page;
- booking section.

---

# Pricing Communication

Hotels must balance transparency and positioning.

Avoid:

- confusing pricing;
- hidden important information;
- unclear offers.

Users should understand value.

---

# Offers and Packages

Professional and competitive.

# Client Presentation Approach

When presenting improvements:

Do not say:

"Your website looks outdated."

Instead say:

"Your website has opportunities to improve guest confidence, increase direct bookings and strengthen the hotel's digital presence."

Focus on business value.
