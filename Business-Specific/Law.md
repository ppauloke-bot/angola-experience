Version: 1.0

Industry: Law Firms, Legal Services & Professional Advisory

# Purpose

This document defines specialized principles for designing and developing digital experiences for law firms and legal professionals.

Supported businesses include:

- Law firms
- Legal advisors
- Corporate lawyers
- Litigation firms
- Specialized legal practices
- Legal consulting companies

---

# Core Principle

Legal websites do not sell legal services.

They sell confidence during important decisions.

Clients are looking for:

- expertise;
- protection;
- reliability;
- strategic guidance.

---

# Business Objectives

A legal website should:

- establish professional authority;
- generate qualified inquiries;
- explain legal expertise;
- build client confidence.

---

# Legal Customer Psychology

Visitors usually ask:

- Can this lawyer help my situation?
- Do they have experience in this area?
- Can I trust them with sensitive information?
- Are they professional?
- What should I do next?

The website should reduce uncertainty.

# Recommended Website Structure

A professional legal website usually includes:

1. Homepage
2. Practice Areas
3. Lawyer Profiles
4. Cases / Experience
5. Insights & Articles
6. About Firm
7. Testimonials
8. Contact / Consultation
9. Locations
10. Careers

---

# Homepage Strategy

The homepage should communicate:

- legal expertise;
- areas of specialization;
- credibility;
- next action.

Important elements:

- clear positioning;
- professional imagery;
- practice areas;
- consultation CTA.

---

# Practice Area Pages

Each legal specialty should have a dedicated page.

Include:

- type of problems handled;
- legal approach;
- expertise;
- relevant experience;
- consultation option.

---

# Explaining Legal Services

Avoid only listing legal terms.

Explain:

- what situations are covered;
- who needs this service;
- how the firm helps.

Clients often do not know the correct legal category.

---

# Lawyer Profiles

People trust professionals, not anonymous companies.

Include:

- name;
- specialization;
- experience;
- education;
- professional memberships;
- publications.

---

# Experience & Case Studies

Legal experience builds confidence.

When appropriate, communicate:

- types of cases handled;
- industries served;
- outcomes achieved.

Respect confidentiality.

---

# Testimonials

Testimonials should focus on:

- professionalism;
- communication;
- trust;
- successful experiences.

Avoid unrealistic guarantees.

---

# Consultation Experience

The contact process should feel safe.

Include:

- clear contact options;
- consultation request forms;
- expected response time.

---

# Confidentiality Communication

Clients need reassurance.

Explain:

- privacy practices;
- confidentiality standards;
- secure communication methods.

---

# Location Strategy

For firms with physical offices:

Show:

- locations;

# Legal Industry Anti-Patterns

Common mistakes include:

---

## Generic Claims

Problem:

Every firm says the same things.

Solution:

Show specific expertise and proof.

---

## Excessive Legal Language

Problem:

Clients feel confused.

Solution:

Explain concepts clearly.

---

## No Human Connection

Problem:

The firm feels anonymous.

Solution:

Show lawyers and real expertise.

---

## Lack of Specialization

Problem:

Visitors do not know if the firm can help.

Solution:

Clearly organize practice areas.

---

## Weak Contact Experience

Problem:

Potential clients hesitate to reach out.

Solution:

Make consultation requests simple.
