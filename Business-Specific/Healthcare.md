# Healthcare

Version: 1.0

Industry: Healthcare & Medical Services

---

# Purpose

This document defines specialized principles for designing and developing trustworthy digital experiences for healthcare organizations.

The goal is to create websites that communicate:

- trust;
- expertise;
- safety;
- accessibility;
- patient confidence.

---

# Core Principle

Healthcare websites are not simple service pages.

They are trust-building systems.

A patient is not only choosing a service.

They are choosing:

- professionals;
- expertise;
- safety;
- confidence.

---

# How Healthcare Experts Think

A healthcare website should answer the patient's most important question:

"Can I trust this organization with my health?"

Every design decision should reinforce:

- credibility;
- transparency;
- professionalism.

---

# Business Objective

The main objective of a healthcare website is to increase:

- appointment requests;
- patient confidence;
- service awareness;
- institutional credibility.

# Recommended Website Structure

A healthcare website usually includes:

1. Homepage
2. Medical Services
3. Doctors / Specialists
4. Conditions & Treatments
5. Patient Information
6. About Organization
7. Facilities
8. Reviews / Testimonials
9. Contact
10. Appointment Booking

The exact structure depends on the healthcare organization.

---

# Homepage Strategy

The homepage should immediately communicate:

- who the organization is;
- what services are offered;
- why patients should trust it;
- how to get help.

---

# Homepage Recommended Flow

## 1. Trust-Focused Hero Section

The first section should communicate:

- medical expertise;
- patient care;
- clear action.

Possible elements:

- professional photography;
- doctor presence;
- appointment CTA.

Avoid:

- generic stock medical images;
- unclear messaging.

---

# 2. Services Overview

Patients should quickly understand:

- available specialties;
- treatments;
- areas of expertise.

Organize information clearly.

---

# 3. Medical Team

Healthcare decisions are strongly influenced by professionals.

Doctor profiles should include:

- name;
- specialty;
- qualifications;
- experience;
- approach to care.

---

# Doctor Profile Strategy

A doctor page should create confidence.

Include:

- professional background;
- areas of expertise;
- education;
- certifications;
- patient-focused information.

Avoid:

- overly technical descriptions;
- missing credentials.

---

# Medical Services Pages

Each service should have its own clear page.

A strong service page explains:

- what it is;
- who it helps;
- what patients can expect;
- available specialists;
- next steps.

---

# Treatment Information

Medical information should be:

- clear;
- accurate;
- understandable.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

---

# Patient Journey Communication

Help patients understand:

Before appointment:

- what to expect;
- required preparation;
- available options.

During care:

- process explanation.

After care:

- follow-up information.

---

# Appointment Experience

Booking an appointment should feel simple and reassuring.

The patient should easily find:

- available contact methods;
- appointment options;
- required information.

---

# Appointment CTA Strategy

Use clear actions:

Examples:

- Schedule Appointment
- Request Consultation
- Contact Our Team

Avoid vague actions.

---

# Appointment Friction

Reduce:

- complicated forms;
- unclear contacts;
- missing information;
- unnecessary steps.

---

# Emergency Information

For hospitals and urgent services:

Important information should be immediately visible.

Examples:

- emergency contacts;
- urgent care information;
- operating hours.

---

# Mobile Healthcare Experience

- readable text;
- quick access to contacts;
- easy appointment actions;
- simple navigation.

# Healthcare Industry Anti-Patterns

Common website problems:

---

## Lack of Trust

Problem:

Patients cannot understand who they are dealing with.

Solution:

Show:

- doctors;
- credentials;
- experience.

---

## Confusing Services

Problem:

Patients do not know what is offered.

Solution:

Create clear service pages.

---

## Poor Patient Communication

Problem:

Information is too technical or unclear.

Solution:

Use patient-focused language.

---

## Hidden Appointments

Problem:

Patients cannot easily contact the organization.

Solution:

Make actions visible.

---

## Outdated Information

Problem:

Incorrect information damages credibility.

Solution:

Maintain accurate content.

---

## Generic Medical Design

Problem:

The website feels impersonal.

Solution:

Build authentic healthcare branding.
