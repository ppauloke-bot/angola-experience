# Purpose

This document defines specialized principles for designing and developing digital experiences for corporate organizations.

Supported businesses include:

- Large companies
- Holding companies
- B2B organizations
- Consulting firms
- Industrial companies
- Corporate groups
- Enterprise service providers

---

# Core Principle

Corporate websites do not primarily sell products.

They sell credibility.

Visitors are evaluating:

- reputation;
- expertise;
- stability;
- professionalism;
- business capability.

---

# Business Objectives

A corporate website should:

- establish authority;
- communicate company value;
- attract business opportunities;
- support recruitment;
- strengthen reputation.

---

# Corporate Customer Psychology

Visitors usually ask:

- Is this company reliable?
- Do they have the necessary expertise?
- Can I trust them as a partner?
- What makes them different?
- What is their track record?

A professional corporate website usually includes:

1. Homepage
2. About Company
3. Services / Solutions
4. Industries
5. Projects / Case Studies
6. Leadership
7. Careers
8. News & Insights
9. Investors
10. Contact

---

# Homepage Strategy

The homepage should communicate:

- company identity;
- main capabilities;
- competitive advantages;
- credibility.

Important elements:

- clear positioning;
- key achievements;
- strategic CTA.

---

# About Company Page

The about page should explain:

- history;
- mission;
- vision;
- values;
- company evolution.

Avoid generic statements.

Show what makes the organization different.

---

# Services & Solutions

Corporate services should be presented through business value.

Explain:

- client challenges;
- company approach;
- expected outcomes.

Avoid only listing capabilities.

---

# Industry Pages

For companies serving multiple sectors, create dedicated industry pages.

Each page should explain:

- industry challenges;
- company expertise;
- relevant solutions.

# Corporate Industry Anti-Patterns

Common mistakes include:

---

## Generic Corporate Messaging

Problem:

The company sounds like every competitor.

Solution:

Communicate specific strengths and proof.

---

## Too Much Internal Language

Problem:

Visitors cannot understand company value.

Solution:

Use customer-focused communication.

---

## Lack of Evidence

Problem:

Claims are unsupported.

Solution:

Show projects, results and achievements.

---

## Outdated Information

Problem:

Reduces credibility.

Solution:

Maintain current content.

---

## Poor Navigation

Problem:

Large companies often have too much information.

Solution:

Create clear information architecture.
